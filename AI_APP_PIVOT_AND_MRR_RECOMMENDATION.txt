AI APP PIVOT AND MRR RECOMMENDATION
Prepared: 5 November 2026

RECOMMENDATION

I would test a pivot into an AI inventory and purchasing planner for a specific
type of Shopify merchant. It offers a plausible path to higher subscription
prices because it helps merchants make recurring decisions involving stock,
cash, and supplier orders. High MRR would still depend on winning and retaining
those merchants.

Assuming you are building with a small team, I would start with beauty and
haircare brands that buy finished products from suppliers.

The promise would be:

"Know what to reorder, when to order it, and how much you can afford -- before
your bestsellers run out."

The Shopify category would become Inventory optimization.
https://apps.shopify.com/categories/orders-and-shipping-inventory-inventory-optimization/all

Your current positioning competes with inexpensive recommendation tools,
including Shopify's free Search & Discovery app:
https://apps.shopify.com/search-and-discovery

Inventory planning has established competitors too. Prediko already offers AI
forecasting and purchase orders, with advertised pricing starting at $49. You
would need a clear advantage for your chosen merchants.
https://apps.shopify.com/prediko


CHANGES I WOULD MAKE

1. TARGET CUSTOMER

Established beauty and haircare brands with meaningful inventory spending,
recurring stockouts, and purchasing managed in spreadsheets.

2. MAIN DASHBOARD

Show products to reorder this week, expected stockout dates, purchasing costs,
and items tying up cash.

3. CORE WORKFLOW

Turn recommendations into a reviewed purchasing plan and draft supplier orders.

4. AI CAPABILITIES

Explain recommendations, interpret supplier documents, identify anomalies, and
answer purchasing questions.

5. PROOF OF VALUE

Track purchasing time, forecast accuracy, stock availability, and excess
inventory.

6. ONBOARDING

Import data, enter supplier constraints, and produce the first useful purchasing
plan.


STRONG INITIAL FEATURE: PURCHASING UNDER A BUDGET

The merchant could ask:

"I have $12,000 available this month. Which products should I replenish first?"

The app would consider:

- Sales demand.
- Current inventory.
- Incoming stock.
- Supplier lead times.
- Minimum order quantities.
- Pack sizes.
- Product margins.

That produces a concrete decision merchants can review every week.

Use forecasting models and validated calculations for quantities. Let the
language model explain the results and help merchants work through them. Show
uncertainty when the data is insufficient.


WHAT YOU CAN REUSE AND WHAT YOU NEED TO BUILD

You can reuse your infrastructure, but this would require substantial new
product work.

KEEP:

- Shopify authentication.
- Tenant isolation.
- PostgreSQL.
- Redis.
- Catalog synchronization infrastructure.
- Background jobs.
- AI provider handling.

BUILD:

- Complete sales-history and inventory synchronization.
- Supplier records, lead times, costs, and purchasing constraints.
- Forecasting, backtesting, and replenishment calculations.
- Purchase-order drafts, approvals, exports, and receiving records.
- Subscription billing and reliable operational monitoring.

Your current catalog cache supports storefront recommendations. It would need a
more complete inventory model.

Older order history also needs additional access. Shopify normally exposes only
the last 60 days, with read_all_orders extending that window under its approval
requirements.
https://shopify.dev/docs/api/usage/access-scopes

I would retire the storefront widgets from the main product experience and make
the purchasing workflow the focus.


PRICING TO TEST

Test willingness to pay before setting permanent plans. A reasonable experiment
would be:

$199 per month:
One store and basic replenishment planning.

$499 per month:
Purchasing constraints, supplier workflows, and richer forecasting.

$999 per month:
Multiple locations and more complex planning.

These are proposed prices to validate.

At an average of $499 per month, 200 retained customers produce $99,800 in
monthly recurring revenue, before expenses. Getting those customers requires
convincing evidence, dependable data, and good support.


VALIDATE BEFORE REBUILDING

Before rebuilding, I would sell a small paid pilot.

1. Interview 10 to 15 qualified merchants.

2. Examine how they currently purchase inventory.

3. Manually produce a sample reorder plan from their data.

4. Aim for three to five paying pilots.

The decisive evidence would be merchants using the plan through repeated
purchasing cycles, renewing, and explaining why they chose it over their
spreadsheet or an existing app.

Acquire those first customers through direct outreach and inventory consultants
or fractional operations managers.

My first move would be to validate that purchasing problem and price with
merchants. That evidence should determine how much of AOVBoost you rebuild.
